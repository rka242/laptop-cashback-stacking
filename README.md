## What "cheap" actually buys you right now

Cheap laptops split into three lanes, and the right one depends on what you do all day.

**Chromebooks** are the value play for browser-first work — email, docs, streaming, student coursework. As a concrete anchor: Walmart has listed the Lenovo Chromebook Plus 15.3" (Intel Core 3, 8GB RAM, 128GB SSD) at $349, down from $469.99. At that price you get a current-gen machine that boots in seconds and doesn't slow down the way old budget Windows laptops do. The tradeoff is obvious: if you need Windows-only software — certain tax tools, legacy Office plugins, PC games — a Chromebook will dead-end you.

**Budget Windows laptops** cover the middle. The Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 3 15.6" with a Ryzen 5, 8GB of RAM and a 512GB SSD has been selling in the $510–$680 range at Best Buy depending on the promo cycle. If a store lists 8GB of RAM at this price, treat it as the floor, not a bonus — several laptops in this lane are upgradeable to 16GB later, which costs less than buying the 16GB configuration upfront in many cases.

**Discounted higher-end machines** are where patient shoppers win. HP's OmniBook 5 carries a $1,300 list price, but HP.com discounts configurations below $700 regularly, sometimes to $550. Wirecutter and CNET reviewers have both noted this pattern — the entry configurations of "premium" laptops dip under $1,000 during sale cycles. If you can wait for one of these dips, you get a better screen and build than any $350 new laptop can offer.

One more lane worth naming: **refurbished**. Dell Refurbished positions itself as the largest direct seller of refurbished Dell computers, and refurbished premium laptops are typically the best longevity-per-dollar in the budget space — last-gen business machines with better keyboards and screens than this year's budget models.

## The mistakes that kill your cash back

Most "my cash back didn't track" stories come from a handful of avoidable errors. These are the ones TopCashback itself flags:

- **Using Safari.** On several major retailers, including Best Buy and Walmart, TopCashback explicitly says it cannot guarantee tracking on Safari. Use Chrome or another browser for the purchase.
- **Clicking through, then wandering off.** If you open the retailer in one tab, then go searching Google for coupon codes in another, the last click before checkout often gets attributed to the coupon site instead — and your cash back dies. Use only coupon codes listed on the TopCashback retailer page itself; unlisted codes void tracking at many stores.
- **Rejecting the cookie consent banner.** Accepting non-essential cookies sounds like a dark pattern, and it is, but those cookies are the same ones affiliate tracking uses. Reject them and the tracking chain can break.
- **Starting on your laptop, finishing on your phone.** The session has to stay in one browser. Buying inside the retailer's app also breaks tracking.
- **Paying with a gift card or stored balance.** Some programs exclude gift card payments entirely. Pay by card where possible.
- **Returning part of the order.** Returns and order modifications claw back cash back proportionally.

None of these are exotic. They're just the fine print that review roundups never spell out, and on a $600 purchase, getting them wrong costs you real money.

One expectation to set: cash back is pending until the retailer confirms it, which happens after the return window closes. For a laptop, figure a few weeks minimum before the money is withdrawable. If a purchase never tracks, TopCashback has a missing cash back claim process — submit it within their stated window rather than assuming it will fix itself.
